Step 3: Decide if you want to double your bet

What Are the Online Blackjack Rules?

Playing online Blackjack is a fun way to pass the time, develop strategic thinking, and practice making decisions under pressure. To get the most enjoyment, you need to know the rules. These 14 key rules will help you play free Blackjack effectively:

14 key rules to follow when playing online Blackjack

  • Your aim is to beat the dealer by reaching a score as close to 21 as possible without going over.
  • If your score exceeds 21, you bust and lose.
  • If your score ties with the dealer, it is a push, and your bet is returned.
  • You place your bet before the cards are dealt.
  • The dealer has one face-up and one face-down card.
  • An Ace counts as 1 or 11, whichever benefits you most.
  • Ten, Jack, Queen, and King each count as 10.
  • Cards 2 through 9 count at face value.
  • Choose "hit" to receive another card.
  • Choose "stand" to end your turn.
  • You may split pairs into two separate hands.
  • The dealer must hit on 16 or less and stand on 17 or more.
  • You may take insurance if the dealer shows an Ace (not recommended).

Five tips to help you play Blackjack well

Tip 1: Learn the basic strategy

Use a basic strategy chart to know when to hit, stand, double, or split based on your hand and the dealer's up-card.

Tip 2: Hit if you are dealt 8 or less

Always hit when your hand totals 8 or lower. There is no risk of busting.

Tip 3: Never split these pairs

Avoid splitting 4s, 5s, and 10s. Splitting these rarely improves your odds.

Tip 4: Never stand on these numbers

If the dealer shows 7 or higher, do not stand on a hard 12 through 16. Hit instead to try improving your hand.

Tip 5: Avoid insurance bets

Insurance side bets generally favor the house and reduce your expected return.

How to Count Cards

Card counting can shift the edge in your favor. Here is the basic four-step Hi-Lo method:

Counting cards in Blackjack in four steps

Step 1: Give every card a value

Step 2: Maintain a running count

Start at zero and add or subtract based on each card dealt. A positive count favors the player; a negative count favors the dealer.

Step 3: Calculate the true count

True count = running count divided by the number of decks remaining in the shoe.

Step 4: Adjust your bets as the true count increases

Raise your bet in proportion to the true count. Higher counts mean a better probability of player advantage.

The History of Blackjack

Many people believe that this casino-style card game takes its name from combining a black (the suit being either a spade or a club) ace and a jack.

However, French card historian Thierry Depaulis has argued that the name is a reference to zinc blende, a mineral linked to silver and gold deposits also known as black-jack.

Depaulis' argument is that 21 was a popular game during the Klondike Gold Rush and that Blackjack evolved from it. The name came about because prospectors used the term "blackjack" to describe the bonus you receive after making 21 from two cards.
